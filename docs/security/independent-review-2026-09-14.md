## Summary

**One High, seven Medium, twenty-two Low, three informational. Nothing Critical: no path to fund loss without a prior eclipse, and no path to a secret.**

The August work held up where it was aimed. Every signing and broadcasting path still passes a binding review of the exact transaction; device authentication fails closed; the Keychain item is add-only, device-bound and group-less; the persisted descriptor is neutered everywhere; the storage root is backup-excluded; the E2E surface is compiled out of Release. Those are recorded under *Verified sound* with citations so the next reader knows they were looked at.

What the August passes did not have in front of them is where the findings cluster:

1. **The peer-consensus rule does not deliver the mitigation the gate report relies on (IR-001, High).** The accepted single-peer-eclipse risk was dispositioned on the strength of "cross-source agreement". The cross-source *set* is built, but the vote is a strict majority unweighted by source, one source class may hold two of three seats, the outvoted peer is banned permanently and struck from the persisted good-peers file, and a wrongly pinned filter header is never re-verified — every later honest answer throws. Two agreeing peers of one class therefore hide payments for the window they control and leave the wallet unable to sync with anyone afterwards, manual honest nodes included, until re-import. This is a design defect independent of who the attacker is; the census work (IR-003, IR-004) is simply the cheapest way to become that attacker.
2. **The census list is an unauthenticated central trust input (IR-003, IR-004, IR-005, Medium/Low).** In Tor mode it is the origin of every automatic peer. It is unsigned, unpinned, published by a workflow token onto an unprotected branch, deployable without validation on a direct push, and imported at release time by hashing whatever bytes arrived. Runtime exposure is bounded — refresh is one manual button, expiry falls back to the bundle — and the publisher/wallet validation really is shared. Signing the document and pinning the generator to a commit closes most of this.
3. **A mid-period checkpoint plus no timestamp rule makes fabricated confirmations free under eclipse at first sync (IR-002, Medium).** Moving the checkpoint to a retarget boundary closes it at no cost.
4. **The new stores are validated but not authenticated (IR-010, Medium).** A substituted signer key in `people.json` becomes an invisible co-owner of shared savings; the register's own hostile-persistence model rated the vault-store analogue High when a semantic check could catch it — here only authenticity can.
5. **Resource bounds on the peer connection are count-bounded where they need to be byte-bounded, and one is not bounded at all (IR-025, IR-026, IR-027, Medium).** The wire framer never releases consumed bytes — reproduced here at +257 MiB resident after 256 MiB of valid frames — so any peer, or a long honest filter scan, can grow one connection past jetsam; the message backlog caps messages, not bytes, so 256 unsolicited 4 MB blocks park a gigabyte per seat; and the seat-0 peer can freeze header sync forever by answering "no headers", silently and unpenalised. None of the nine fuzz targets or the sanitizer runs can see any of these, which is why they survived the August evidence.
6. **Hardening in the post-audit code (IR-011 – IR-024, IR-028 – IR-033, Low/Info).** The vault paths sit outside the `spending` gate; a post-broadcast commit failure is swallowed; card import skips the key-network check the builder performs; names and cards are unbounded; the seed-bearing export staging file is weaker than the Keychain item it copies and is never swept; `redactedPreview` fails open; several stores have no pinned protection class.

Governance and evidence (IR-007, IR-008, IR-009): the audit manifest describes a tree that no longer exists, the egress inventory is three entries short, and the written gate still says NO-GO while mainnet is the shipped default with no recorded decision. None of these is a vulnerability; all of them are the kind of drift the register's own rules exist to prevent, and a reader of `docs/security/` today is misled by them.

**What this review is not.** It is a read of the source at one revision by an agent the owner engaged, with parallel sub-reviews whose every conclusion was re-traced by the reviewer before entering this file. No test was written, no mutation was run, no device was touched, and `SEC-001`–`SEC-028` were taken as closed. The gate report's outstanding item — an uninvolved human auditor — remains outstanding.

| IR-001 | **High** | S5 | **Two peers of one source class outvote and permanently ban the third, and the filter state they pin can never be displaced by honest peers.** `DiversityPolicy.admits` is a ceiling: with `peerCount` 3 one class may hold 2 seats (`PeerDiversity.swift:110-118`). `FilterSync` adopts a *strict majority* of cfcheckpt and cfheaders answers (`FilterSync.swift:371-409`, `:546-554`) and calls `pool.misbehaving` on the minority, which disconnects, bans for the session, and strikes the endpoint from the persisted good-peers file (`PeerPool.swift:219-226`). Replacements dialled mid-scan are deliberately never consulted (`FilterSync.swift:252-258`, `:272-279`), so after the eviction the sync runs on the two agreeing peers, which also serve the cfilters (`:280-283`), and the headers they pin are persisted (`:284-288`). Once pinned, every later cfcheckpt (`checkPinnedBoundaries`, `:415-424`) and every batch anchor (`anchorPreviousHeader`, `:607-611`) **throws** on a disagreeing answer rather than re-verifying; nothing in `Sources/WinnowApp` handles `checkpointMismatch`/`filterHeaderMismatch`, and `filters.json` is removed only on wallet adopt or delete (`AppModel.swift:1163`, `:1319`); `rollBack` prunes only above a header-chain fork (`FilterSync.swift:806-817`). Consequence: an attacker who seats 2 of the 3 automatic peers for one sync (a) scans that window with filters that hide the victim's payments, and (b) leaves the wallet unable to sync with *any* honest peer afterwards — three honest manual nodes included — until the wallet is re-imported. This defeats the mitigation named for the accepted single-peer-eclipse risk (`gate-report.md:109-124`, "cross-source agreement"): the cross-source *set* is built (`FilterSync.swift:458-473`) but the vote is not weighted by source, so both agreeing peers may be the same class. In Tor mode both the onion and clearnet automatic lists are census-derived `.fallback` (`PeerPool.swift:716-727`; DNS is disabled unless `.direct`, `SeedResolver.swift:57`). Not Critical: headers stay PoW-checked and blocks merkle-verified, so this cannot fabricate confirmations or reach a secret. | Traced as cited. Evidence gap noted by the reviewer: `FilterSyncAdversaryTests.swift:42,193-195` states that "two liars would agree and form a majority for the lie" but no test exercises two same-class liars against one honest cross-class peer, and no test exercises recovery from a wrongly pinned filter header. **Fix direction:** (1) treat a majority drawn from a single source class as *no majority* — do not ban, do not persist; (2) on a pinned-vs-announced mismatch, drop pinned headers back to the last checkpoint that was corroborated across classes and re-verify instead of throwing; (3) make `misbehaving` from a filter vote session-scoped rather than striking the persisted file, or require cross-class corroboration before striking. |
| IR-002 | Medium | S5 | **A mid-period checkpoint leaves the first retarget unchecked, and no timestamp rule exists, so an attacker who eclipses a wallet at first sync can extend the chain at difficulty 1.** `expectedBits` returns `nil` when the period-first header is unavailable and `requireDifficulty` then skips the check (`HeaderChain.swift:232-257`). The mainnet checkpoint is at 900,000 (`NetworkParams.swift:222-230`); 900,000 mod 2016 = 864, so the adjustment at 901,152 has `firstIndex = −864` and is unchecked — documented at `HeaderChain.swift:56-59`. Timestamps are not validated at all (`:60`). An eclipsing peer may therefore serve a header at 901,152 with `bits = powLimit` (any target ≤ powLimit is accepted, `:263-274`) and hold difficulty 1 thereafter by spacing timestamps exactly 600 s (elapsed == timespan → unchanged). A fabricated block then costs 2^32 hashes — seconds on one GPU — against ~2^78 for an honest one, so under eclipse the attacker can show any number of confirmations for any transaction at no hash cost. Precondition that keeps this Medium: the wallet must hold no honest work past 901,152 (fresh install, or a rebuilt header file); a chain already carrying honest work cannot be displaced, because a replacement branch must carry strictly more work (`:363-367`). | Retarget arithmetic otherwise matches Core exactly (period-first at h−2016, previous at h−1, clamp [ts/4, ts×4], powLimit cap, `:248-257`). `scripts/refresh-checkpoint` and `CheckpointGenerator.swift` accept any height; nothing enforces a boundary. **Fix:** ship the checkpoint at a height ≡ 0 (mod 2016) — `firstIndex` then resolves to the checkpoint header itself and the gap closes for free — and enforce `height % 2016 == 0` in the generator. Separately consider Core's future-drift rule (time ≤ now + 2 h) so timestamp lies cannot ease difficulty 4× per period. |

### Hostile input — verified sound

- Every wire count cap precedes allocation: payload ≤ 4,000,000 checked before `total` and before the `subdata` (`Framing.swift:59-62`); headers ≤ 2,000 (`Messages.swift:415-417`); inv/getdata/notfound ≤ 50,000 (`Inventory.swift:64-66`); cfheaders ≤ 2,000, cfcheckpt ≤ 100,000 (`Messages.swift:199,264`); addr ≤ 1,000 (`:393-395`); block transactions ≤ 100,000 (`Block.swift:126-128`); transaction inputs/outputs bounded by remaining bytes, witness items ≤ 1,000, var-data ≤ 4,000,000 (`Transaction.swift:125,143,155,162,173,175`); GCS filter n ≤ 1,000,000 with the minimum-bits check before any loop (`GCSFilter.swift:73-77,95-98`). A `headers` entry's `txn_count` is read as a minimal varint and must be 0 (`Messages.swift:420-421`, `Wire.swift:74-92`).
- `CensusCatalog`: 4 MiB before `JSONDecoder`; exact key set; per-overlay counts; host ≤ 255 bytes; every fixed index (`bytes[32..34]`, IPv4/IPv6 octets) sits behind a length guard in the same short-circuiting `guard` or comes from a fixed-size `in_addr`/`in6_addr`; `decodeBase32` shifts a `UInt32` with ≤ 12 pending bits; `OnionChecksum.hash` always pads to at least input + 1.
- `SenderLookup`: 2 MiB re-checked after fetch; root must be an object with `vin` ≤ 10,000; every address re-validated by the fuzzed `AddressDecoder`. `FundingSources.pushedItems` checks `endIndex − index ≥ count` before every `subdata`; `Taproot.ControlBlock` enforces `33 + 32m, m ≤ 128`; the P256K x-only tweak-add *throws* on an off-curve x rather than trapping and is consumed with `try?`.
- The trap grep (`fatalError | precondition( | try! | as! | .first! | .last! | [0]`) over the network, funding, people, label, lookup and app sources found only constructor invariants on lengths the decoder already fixed, a Release stub, and indexes behind real guards — e.g. `FilterSync.swift:374` reads `checkpoints[0]` when `count ≤ 1`, and `collectedCheckpoints` throws on an empty list before it is reached (`:346-349`). No trap reachable from peer, file, HTTP or pasted bytes was found.
- Low-difficulty header chains cannot consume memory: every staged header must carry `bits == expected` and real work before the next is read (`HeaderChain.swift:248-257,355-358`); a no-progress batch is a replay, at most four per sync (`:397-401,440`), and any other data fault evicts the peer.
- `MempoolWindow`: `seenTxids` ≤ 10,000 FIFO, `inFlight` ≤ 512, 30 s expiry (`MempoolWindow.swift:178-187,288-296`). `TxBroadcaster`: pending ≤ 4,096, raw tx ≤ 4 MB, file ≤ 64 MiB, attempts ≤ 63, schedule ≤ 366 d (`TxBroadcaster.swift:162-168,490-527`).
- Tor bridge SOCKS handshake: `Buffer::new_precise()` is 1,024 bytes and `recv.buf()` returns only the deficit slice, so the socket cannot over-read and `note_received`'s panic precondition is unreachable (tor-socksproto 0.46.0).

### Secret containment — verified sound

- The Keychain item is add-only (`SecItemAdd`, throws on duplicate rather than overwriting), `WhenUnlockedThisDeviceOnly`, `kSecAttrSynchronizable = false`, in the app's own default access group — no entitlements file, no app groups, no `SecItemUpdate` anywhere, no weaker accessibility constant anywhere (`KeychainStore.swift:22-29,51-58`). Observed, not merely read, by `AppTests/KeychainAttributeTests.swift:55-57`.
- The persisted wallet descriptor is built from the *neutered* account key on every path (`Wallet.swift:645,675,680-686`; `ImportBundle.swift:465`), so `wallet.json` and `AppModel.walletDescriptor` never carry an xprv; the master key is derived per call and never held in state (`Wallet.swift:1525-1545`, `AppModel.swift:2272-2282`).

The bundle format carries the mnemonic only as an explicit opt-in on the encoder side (`ImportBundle.swift:276`, `Wallet.swift:1588-1589`).

## Verified sound

Recorded so that a later reader knows what was looked at and found correct, not only what was found wrong.

- **Retarget arithmetic** matches Core's `pow.cpp`: period-first at h−2016 and previous at h−1 (the 2015-interval span), clamp, powLimit cap, and a wrapping multiply that cannot overflow because powLimit × 4 × timespan < 2^256 (`HeaderChain.swift:232-257`, `UInt256.swift:120-132`). `UInt256.target(compact:)` mirrors `SetCompact` including the negative and overflow rules and does not trap for any 32-bit input (`UInt256.swift:172-185`).
- **Fork choice:** equal work is refused; both the append and the replace path PoW-check and difficulty-check every header (`HeaderChain.swift:316-376`).
- **CI:** every action is SHA-pinned; triggers are `pull_request`, never `pull_request_target`; the self-hosted jobs are gated on a same-repository head (`ci.yml:59`, `node-tests.yml:74,150`); signing secrets are step-scoped in the release and App Store lanes.
- **Tor bridge** (`TorBridge/src/lib.rs`): loopback-only listener bound to port 0, non-loopback peers dropped, 64-stream permit cap, CONNECT only, `.i2p` refused, 30 s handshake and 120 s connect timeouts, an isolated Tor client per stream. Two notes rather than findings: iOS has no per-app loopback isolation, so any foregrounded app can use the SOCKS proxy while Winnow runs (it proxies; it does not expose wallet data); `panic = "abort"` in `Cargo.toml` means any Rust panic terminates the app.
- **Tor-mode DNS:** `SeedResolver.routed` returns nothing unless the route is `.direct` (`SeedResolver.swift:57`), so seed hostnames never leave via system DNS in Tor mode.
- **ATS:** only `NSAllowsLocalNetworking` (for the loopback proxy), no arbitrary loads; no `UIBackgroundModes` (`project.yml:26-27,83`).
- **Census refresh runtime hygiene:** manual trigger only, 4 MiB cap enforced on `Content-Length` and while streaming, ephemeral session with no cookies or cache, invalid or expired documents never reach the pool.
